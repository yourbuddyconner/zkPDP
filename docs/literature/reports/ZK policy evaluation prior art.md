# Prior art leaves zk-pdp narrow openings

**zk-pdp is not novel as an idea, and it looks novel only as a specific build.** Moving an authorization decision to an untrusted party and checking a proof is a 1999 idea ([Appel and Felten](https://doi.org/10.1145/319709.319718)), and a peer-reviewed system already proves XACML decisions in RISC Zero ([Prezta](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11466)). Of the seven claims examined, the umbrella claim and "denials are provable" are already done, Cedar in a zkVM and the proof of compilation are partly done, and three things appear open: compiling Cedar to circuits through the Term IR, a same-policy zkVM versus circuit comparison, and the exact fixed-shape Merkle record with ancestor closure. The AuthZEN carrier is also unclaimed, but the spec makes it easy enough that a reviewer will call it obvious. The two strongest contributions are the Cedar compiler and the comparison, and only the compiler has evidence behind it today: **no zkVM proof has finished, the compilation proof is unbuilt, and the AuthZEN end-to-end test has not run** (`results/E1-prove-2026-11-04.jsonl` is empty; E3, E5 and E7 say "planned"). Every "first" below rests on searches that the notes themselves show to be leaky, so each must be worded "we found no" and rechecked before submission. This niche filled monthly through 2026, and a public proposal to run Cedar in a zkVM predates the project by four weeks ([Warrant](https://github.com/mnemonik-xyz/warrant/blob/main/proposals/verified-intent-chain.md)). The merged bibliography holds **423 works: 30 read-full, 177 read-abstract, 216 search-summary-only**, and no key paper has been read cover to cover. The notes record no reading by a person at all.

### Claim 0: the idea itself is old

**Sources establish.** Appel and Felten had clients submit proofs because their logic has no decision procedure ([CCS 1999](https://doi.org/10.1145/319709.319718)). Bauer's web system put it plainly: "All the server needs to do is verify that the client's proof is valid", and it already bound proofs to a session to stop theft ([TR-638-01](http://users.ece.cmu.edu/~lbauer/papers/pcaprototr.pdf)). Evidence-based audit put proofs in logs as "a receipt" in 2008 ([Vaughan et al.](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~stevez/papers/VJMZ08.pdf)). With succinct proofs, Prezta proves XACML decisions at about 14 or 28 s, 238 to 250 KB, 14 to 15.5 ms to verify, and passes 323 of 389 testable conformance cases; it maps NotApplicable, Deny and Indeterminate all to false and says it uses "not zero-knowledge" ([Prezta](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11466)). In 2016 Cinderella compiled X.509 policy templates to C and on to verifiable computation ([paper](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cinderella.pdf)).

**Inference.** The defensible difference from proof-carrying authorization is narrow: succinctness, hiding of premises, and proof of non-monotonic outcomes (forbid-overrides, default deny, Deny itself) over a committed closed world. The authorization-lineage researcher found no source stating that third part, so it is the project's argument to make. None of the 2026 agent papers cites this lineage, so connecting the two is a real contribution to a related-work section.

### Claim 1: Cedar in a zkVM is new only in the most literal sense

**Sources establish.** Newton runs a Regorus fork with policy as data in an SP1 challenge circuit, though its core assurance is a BLS operator quorum and its circuit repository is not public, so no proving numbers were verified ([newton-contracts](https://github.com/newt-foundation/newton-contracts), [crate](https://docs.rs/newton-rego-kernel/latest/newton_rego_kernel/)). Warrant's proposal, merged 2026-10-07, says "Run the Rust `cedar-policy` evaluator (DRT-tested) in the guest" and also "Nothing in this document is implemented" ([proposal](https://github.com/mnemonik-xyz/warrant/blob/main/proposals/verified-intent-chain.md)). Atlas translates a Cedar subset to its own predicates ahead of a RISC Zero guest, failing closed on arithmetic and sets ([bridge](https://github.com/Atlas-Authorization/pca/tree/main/packages/pca-policy-bridge)). ERC-8354 requires "a policy interpreter, not a compiled policy" and names no language ([ERC-8354](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/ERCS/erc-8354.md)). In the project's own record, `cedar-policy` does not build unchanged, so `stacker` is stubbed through `[patch.crates-io]` (`docs/decisions/0015-stacker-stand-in.md`), and one composite proof was stopped unfinished at 28 minutes (`docs/experiments/E1-cedar-in-risc0.md`).

**Inference.** Scaling Prezta's 3.8 s for 2^15 cycles to 23.3 million cycles gives about 45 minutes on 16 vCPUs, so 28 unfinished minutes is unsurprising; this is rough and ignores segment effects. A source note added during this write-up reaches a lower figure from a later benchmark's 21 kHz: about 18.5 minutes on an M4 Pro, and it suggests E1's runs may simply have been stopped too early (`docs/sources/S27-sok-understanding-zkvm.md`). Neither estimate is a measurement. **Null result.** `cedar_policy risc0_zkvm` hit only this project, `cedar_policy sp1_zkvm` hit nothing, none of the 89 `cedar-policy` reverse dependencies is named for a ZK system (name level only), and Cedar's organization shows no ZK discussion. Jolt and OpenVM got only generic queries.

### Claim 2: the compiler is the most defensible contribution

**Sources establish.** Policy-to-circuit compilation is well trodden in smaller languages. ZK-TPL compiles a Prolog-like language to ZoKrates and Groth16, with per-policy setup and no stated conformance test ([ZK-TPL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.02454)). zkGen transpiles a JSON policy language to circuits, reports transpile time only, and says "the zkPolicy language lacks a formal definition" ([zkGen](https://tum-esi.github.io/publications-list/PDF/2024-ICBC-zkGen.pdf)). Circuitree produces R1CS from Datalog for Bulletproofs ([Circuitree](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/288551/1/published-Circuitree_A_Datalog_Reasoner_in_Zero-Knowledge.pdf)). zk-odrl's generic circuit is "specified ... not built yet" ([zk-odrl](https://github.com/dedonnodev/zk-odrl)), and zk-Guard-R specifies a bounded policy interpreter but measures a simpler circuit ([zk-Guard-R](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26165045)). OpenAC's design note keeps one circuit with policy as a public boolean expression and contains no circuits ([README](https://github.com/privacy-ethereum/zkID/blob/main/generalized-predicates/README.md)). CirC is the technical twin: one IR lowering to both SMT and R1CS ([CirC](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10432512)); its field-blaster is verified only up to bit-width and arity 4, and that work found 4 bugs ([Ozdemir et al.](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/778)). Others use SymCC only for analysis, ending at SMT ([decern-proof](https://github.com/anivar/decern/blob/main/crates/decern-proof/src/lib.rs)).

The front end's assurance has limits the project's wording does not yet carry. SymCert verifies a Lean symbolic compiler over a 42-operator term language, trusts the solver, and targets SMT analyses on Cedar 4.4 ([SymCert](https://cdn.amazon.science/9f/c9/5d18658a41c48628b686493aa327/scipub-approval152134-46496997-symcert-verifying-smtbased-policy-analyses.pdf)). A secondary source says "The Rust `cedar-policy-symcc` crate is only DRT-tested" ([Warrant](https://github.com/mnemonik-xyz/warrant/blob/main/proposals/verified-intent-chain.md)). The project reads its terms from that Rust crate through the last assertion of `never_matches_asserts`, pinned to 0.8.1 (`docs/decisions/0019-compiler-design.md`), and its zkVM guest uses Cedar 4.14.1 (`docs/decisions/0015-stacker-stand-in.md`). No note checked how the proofs relate to these versions. Differential testing also cannot see missing constraints: a same-budget valid-execution baseline exposed none of LIEZZ's accepted-witness soundness failures, and one of its Noir bugs sits in Barretenberg lowering ([LIEZZ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30648)). Noir's own checks only look for "some (indirect) connection" ([source](https://github.com/noir-lang/noir/blob/master/compiler/noirc_evaluator/src/ssa/checks/check_for_missing_brillig_constraints.rs)).

**Inference.** The verified boundary stops at the Term IR, and in the Rust artifact it is differentially tested, not proved. Whether SymCert's theorems transfer to a non-SMT consumer is unread and unlikely to be automatic. ERC-8354 argues directly against compiled policy, and the compilation proof is the answer, so claims 2 and 4 are stronger together. Cinderella's chain has the same shape and should be cited early. **Null result.** `cedar-policy-symcc noir` and `cedar-policy-symcc circuit` hit only this project and unrelated files, and several searches found no Cedar or Rego compiler to circuits. An unread vendor blog on compiling agent policy to an "evaluation circuit" ([OpenMatter](https://openmatter.substack.com/p/enforcing-policy-with-a-circuit)) needs a person's check.

### Claim 3: the carrier is unclaimed and close to trivial

**Sources establish.** AuthZEN 1.0 leaves `context` format to implementers, and says "The PDP MAY choose to sign its authorization response", with no mechanism ([sections 5.5.2, 11.6](https://openid.net/specs/authorization-api-1_0.html)). Signed portable decisions go back at least to 2014: "A Web Service MAY use this token to determine that a trusted 3rd party has evaluated an XACML Request Context" ([XACML SAML Profile](https://docs.oasis-open.org/xacml/3.0/xacml-profile-saml2.0-v2-spec-en.html)). Atlas verifies a signed object in the request `context` and returns a boolean ([pca-authzen](https://github.com/Atlas-Authorization/pca/tree/main/packages/pca-authzen)). Issue 656 asks for a signed response "where a PDP decision needs to be verified beyond the immediate PEP" ([issue 656](https://github.com/openid/authzen/issues/656)). The Farley draft signs decisions, claims no correctness, and requires only that a separate ZK proof bind to `committed_fields_root` ([draft](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-farley-acta-signed-receipts-01.html)).

**Sources also contradict a project claim.** Section 5.5 says "If the PEP does not understand information in the context response object, the PEP MAY choose to reject the decision" ([spec](https://openid.net/specs/authorization-api-1_0.html)). Section 10.1.1's ignore-unknown rule is general and 5.5 is specific to `context` on a permit.

**Inference.** "No spec change" holds; "no behavior change" depends on the implementation. An unmodified PEP gains nothing from a proof it ignores, so this is compatibility and not security. Section 11.6's claim that signing "ensures response accuracy" is the best quotable motivation, and issue 656 is a live opening worth more in practice than a novelty claim. **Null result.** `repo:openid/authzen zero knowledge` and `zk` returned 0 issues, and two searches found no draft defining a proof of correct evaluation in a response. Most drafts were seen only as summaries, and the OpenID mailing list and meeting notes were not searched.

### Claim 4: compilation proofs have two near precedents, and neither is this

**Sources establish.** vkproof names the threat, that a maliciously generated verifying key makes verifiers accept proofs of a different functionality, and gives a roughly 1.7 KB argument that a Varuna key matches a program's R1CS. It has no implementation, and it leaves open whether modular compilation fits a real language ([vkproof](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/581)). Ron and Monperrus run ChibiCC in RISC Zero, median 78.91 s, verification about 49 s, with the compiler image ID pinned, and they say this establishes provenance, not correctness ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11887)). Leo emits per-run ACL2 proofs, which others call partial, and its end-to-end proof is future work ([Coglio et al.](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1278), [Ozdemir et al.](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/778)). GordieHowe's credential compiler emits a Lean certificate per program that its optimizations preserve semantics, though its language is for writing relations by hand and has no policy compiler in the sections read ([ePrint 2026/2615](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2615), `docs/sources/S26-gordiehowe.md`). Trident's "verify the proof of compilation" is a roadmap line ([Trident](https://github.com/cyberia-to/trident/blob/master/reference/roadmap.md)). Deployed systems use allowlists or registries instead: Prezta signs its image ID, Aztec checks a registry root ([Aztec](https://docs.aztec.network/developers/docs/foundational-topics/contract_creation)). Tan et al. show 48 of 88 known compiler bugs make an SP1 guest commit a wrong output ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04701)).

**Inference.** The statement proved is "this pinned compiler, on input H, produced K". It proves provenance, and a wrong compiler yields a validly proven wrong key, so "compiler correctness" overstates it. A per-program certificate in GordieHowe's style answers the other question, whether the output is faithful, so a reviewer will ask why the project does not use one; the two could combine. If the policy is public and the toolchain deterministic, a verifier could recompute K, so the proof mostly saves work for constrained verifiers. The saving is key distribution and not policy trust, because `docs/protocol.md` still allowlists `policy_hash`. The real risk is cost: UltraHonk key generation includes curve commitments, and nobody has shown Noir compilation or Barretenberg in a guest. E5's fallback, proving only the Noir source translation, would be a weaker claim. Until it runs, this has the same standing as Warrant's and Trident's unbuilt statements. **Null result.** `proof of compilation zk`, `verifiable compilation zkvm` and `compiler zkvm verification key` returned 0 repositories, but `nargo risc0 guest compile circuit` and `circom compiler zkvm guest prove compilation` were rate limited and did not complete.

### Claim 5: the commitment is distinctive only when stated narrowly

**Sources establish.** Proving a denial is not new: Prezta's guest commits `decision` either way, and ERC-8354's envelope carries DENY, though its reference circuit "only proves ALLOW verdicts" ([main.nr](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/assets/erc-8354/circuits/src/main.nr)). The nearest idea is Warrant's roadmap: "The guest checks Merkle inclusion and slice completeness before it evaluates", unbuilt, with an open question on slicing preconditions ([proposal](https://github.com/mnemonik-xyz/warrant/blob/main/proposals/verified-intent-chain.md)). ZKMB already does Merkle non-membership for blocklists ([ZKMB](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-grubbs.pdf)). Precomputed closure and authenticated non-membership are each old ([US 10230733](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10230733), search summary only; [Catalano et al.](https://www.iacr.org/archive/eurocrypt2008/49650430/49650430.pdf)). Prezta has no entity store, so no relationship lookup ([Prezta](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11466)). The project's record shows the limits: 8 attribute and 16 ancestor slots, and no support for a principal or resource absent from the store (`docs/decisions/0019-compiler-design.md`). The attribute authority must compute closure honestly (`docs/threat-model.md`).

**Inference.** The distinctive form is a denial in a per-policy circuit, against a committed store, by positional non-membership, so the prover cannot pick a favorable subset. A reviewer may call the parts obvious. A proof over an old root is a proof of an old truth, which is Zanzibar's "new enemy" problem ([Pang et al.](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc19-pang.pdf)), and key transparency already solved split views ([CONIKS](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity15/technical-sessions/presentation/melara)). The project's documents do not mention either. **Null result.** Web queries on denial with Merkle non-membership and on authenticated closure returned nothing that connects the two in a circuit for policy evaluation. These were few queries, in general web search only.

### Claim 6: the comparison looks clearly original, and is not yet earned

**Sources establish.** Sheybani et al. run one matrix and one SHA-256 row each, an unreviewed preprint, and RISC Zero beats Noir on SHA-256 while losing on matrices ([Table IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.07063)). csp-benchmarks gives a same-machine table for ECDSA and SHA-256 ([results](https://github.com/privacy-ethereum/csp-benchmarks/tree/main/results)). Gassmann et al. compare zkVMs against CPUs, not circuits ([ASPLOS 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3779212.3790159)). Parsing dominance appears in one vendor figure, 50 percent of proving cost at one point in a Monad client ([Category Labs](https://www.category.xyz/blogs/optimizing-the-monad-client-for-zkvm-execution)). The project measures 50.3 percent schema, 40.3 percent entities, 8.6 percent policies and 0.3 percent authorize over 23.3 million cycles (`docs/experiments/E1-cedar-in-risc0.md`).

**Inference.** The split between compiling and interpreting policy is live across Prezta, Newton, ERC-8354 and zk-odrl, but no one has measured it for one language, so this is the clearest original contribution. Direction depends on whether a workload has a precompile, so one workload cannot settle it. The honest statement today is a lower bound, over 28 minutes against about 2.4 s, with the zkVM reading the whole store and the circuit two records. No ratio should be printed. Coverage must sit next to any speed claim: all of Cedar in the zkVM against 3 of 7 upstream sets. **Null result.** No peer-reviewed comparison was found. The notes could not read the two ACM papers most likely to hold one, but source notes added during this write-up report that [SoK: Understanding zkVM](https://doi.org/10.1145/3779208.3805964) has no circuit arm and [Zero-Knowledge VMs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3832783.3837500) is not a performance paper (`docs/sources/S27-sok-understanding-zkvm.md`, `docs/sources/S28-zkvms-security-usability.md`). Those are key-section reads, and they strengthen this null without closing it.

The paper should not say any of these: first policy engine in a zkVM (Newton, Prezta); first policy compiler to circuits (Cinderella, ZK-TPL, zkGen, Circuitree); first proof of denial (Prezta, Warrant); first to think of Cedar in a zkVM (Warrant); a verified compiler end to end; a Lean-verified evaluator; "unmodified" without the `stacker` footnote; "no behavior change" at an unmodified PEP; compiler correctness from a compilation proof; "extends to arbitrary Cedar policies"; any zkVM-to-circuit ratio; or "zero-knowledge" unqualified.

That last point needs its own care. RISC Zero says "we have not written a mathematical argument to prove that our system is zero-knowledge" ([security model](https://dev.risczero.com/api/security-model)), its advisory is open with no patch ([GHSA-5xgj-pmjj-gw49](https://github.com/risc0/risc0/security/advisories/GHSA-5xgj-pmjj-gw49)), and its docs say the Groth16 wrap does not run on Apple silicon ([docs](https://dev.risczero.com/api/generating-proofs/local-proving)), although one benchmark reports a Groth16 stage on an M4 Pro (`docs/sources/S27-sok-understanding-zkvm.md`). The Haböck and Kindi note gives honest-verifier theorems for specific protocols, reports gaps in RISC Zero's design that it says were patched, reports no exploit, and gives no RISC Zero parameters, so it neither shows RISC Zero is zero-knowledge nor shows it is not (`docs/sources/S25-haboeck-kindi.md`). UltraHonk's ZK mode is designed to hide the witness, no published proof was found, and before v5.0.0 it had a flaw permitting forged ZK proofs ([zkVerify PR 441](https://github.com/zkVerify/zkVerify/pull/441), [Certora](https://www.certora.com/blog/payy-exploit-forged-zk-proof-attack)). Noir's `SECURITY.md` at 1.0.0 says "not recommended for use in production" ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/noir-lang/noir/blob/v1.0.0/SECURITY.md)). So, by the notes' inference, the project's laptop has no RISC Zero configuration with proven zero-knowledge, subject to settling the Groth16 conflict.

A reviewer will also press where the work is not needed. For a vendor-hosted decision point in a well-run data center, a TEE is cheaper and covers all of Cedar: about 98 ms to attest and 5 ms to verify in one agent system, not a PDP ([Proof-of-Guardrail](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05786v1)). The project's case holds where the decider controls the hardware, since interposers now extract attestation keys ([TEE.fail](https://tee.fail/)). Where the verifier may hold the policy and facts, re-deciding wins: Cedar decides in microseconds ([Cutler et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04651)) against 20 ms to verify. Simple credential predicates are better served by specialist systems at 65 to 500 ms for a full mDL presentation on desktop or server hardware ([Vega](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Vega_Low-Latency-Zero-Knowledge-Proofs-over-Existing-Credentials.pdf), [GordieHowe](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2615)). As specified, the private mode also lacks unlinkability, device binding, revocation and issuer hiding. Its best citation for the problem is Bormann and Lehmann, who say practical systems reach efficiency by hard-coding a policy, credential format and signature scheme (`docs/sources/S29-sok-anonymous-credentials.md`).

## No key paper has been read in full by a person

The notes record no human reading at all. Only Prezta's arXiv v2 body comes close, with its appendices not read closely and the USENIX version not compared. The `readdetail` field in the bibliography keeps what each note actually said.

**Seven source notes changed while this report was being written.** At about 21:19 to 21:20 local time on 2026-11-04, `docs/sources/S19` was updated and `S24` to `S29` appeared, written from PDFs the human supplied. They record an assistant's key-section reads and no human reading, and they are not part of the assigned notes folder. I used them only where they change a verdict, and I cite them by file. ## Conclusion

The search changed what the project should treat as its contribution. The spec's risk list imagines Prezta publishing a Cedar variant, but the nearer threats are less formal and already public: a merged proposal naming the same zkVM step, an Ethereum draft on the project's own proof stack, and an open AuthZEN issue asking for exactly this kind of decision. Priority over any of these is measured in weeks, so the durable claims are empirical, not conceptual: the compiler's coverage number, the same-policy comparison, and the parsing measurement. Those survive a competing proposal, and "we thought of it first" does not.

The next useful acts are cheap relative to their effect on the paper: have a person read SymCert's theorem statements, let one like-for-like zkVM proof run to the end, add adversarial-witness tests to the compiler, and rerun the searches through ACM DL, IEEE Xplore and DBLP before submission. The adversarial-witness tests matter most, because the project's strongest claim is a compiler whose only stated check cannot see the failure mode that source after source reports for ZK compilers.
