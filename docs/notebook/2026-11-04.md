## What happened

The human brought a four-line idea note:

> ZK Policy Decision point. Run Cedar in a zkVM. Provide AuthZen + Proof.
> Could do client-side policy decisions.

The assistant researched it and the idea changed shape over three exchanges.
This entry records how, because the reasoning is the provenance.

### Round 1: research on the idea as stated

Searches found Prezta (S01), the direct precedent: XACML in RISC Zero at
USENIX Security 2026. Nothing combining Cedar with a zkVM. AuthZEN 1.0 Final
since January (S02), with an open response context and an ignore-unknown-fields
rule. Cedar's Lean verification (S04). RISC Zero's zero-knowledge advisory
(S08). Noisy vendor cost claims (S20).

The assistant's assessment, which the human did not dispute: the value is not
"proving a PDP ran". It is letting an untrusted party decide and still be
believed. The hard problem is not evaluation but input provenance, which is
where Prezta spent its cycles. Proving is too slow for a request path, so the
model is prove once and present many. In a trusted single-organization
deployment a signature is cheaper and the paper must say so.

### Round 2: the human asked about a Cedar evaluator in Noir

The assistant's answer: a general interpreter in a circuit is a zkVM written
badly. A compiler from validated policy sets to circuits is sane, because
validated Cedar is static and error-free, and AWS's symbolic compiler (S05)
already produces a typed Term IR from exactly that. Strings become bounded
arrays, the hierarchy becomes a Merkle-committed closure, policy changes become
new verification keys. Noir's client-side proving (S14, S15, S16) is the reason
to do it.

### Round 3: the human chose both

The human: try both, benchmark and compare. Prefers the zkVM's general
properties. Would settle for the compiler. Thinks they compose. Noted that Noir
would be fast server-side too.

The assistant's refinements: compose at the verifier through shared
commitments, and prove compilation in the zkVM so the circuit backend gets
policy as data back. Server-side the batch endpoint favors the zkVM. The
comparison is only meaningful if both are backends of one envelope and one
harness. Order: zkVM first, hand circuit second, compiler third.

### Round 4: spec the project as research

The human asked for this repository, planned as a paper, with provenance of
decisions, reasoning and research. They declined the brainstorming questions
and said "ship it". Everything in this repository as of this entry was written
by the assistant from the conversation above, and the decision records mark
which choices were the human's and which were the assistant's batch-accepted
proposals.

## Evening: GPU proving on the laptop, a dead end

Hours, assistant: zkVM backend about 1.5 more. The human asked for any
acceleration available, then to stop if it cost too much.

- The human saw a flat GPU graph during a run and doubted the GPU was used. Correct. In `risc0-circuit-rv32im` 4.0.5 the Metal branch of `segment_prover()` is commented out. Only CUDA or CPU is reachable. The `metal` feature of `risc0-zkvm` 3.0.6 is `["prove"]` and nothing more. The crate docs still say Metal is on by default.
- The human found upstream issue risc0/risc0#3753, which says the same and adds that `risc0-circuit-recursion` and `risc0-circuit-keccak` are CPU only too.
- A third-party hybrid patch in that issue measures about 1.7 times on an M4 Max, because constraint evaluation, 87 percent of a proof, stays on the CPU. Not tried: unreviewed prover code for a small gain.
- Upstream PR 3688, "Enable metal support", merged to `main` on 2026-01-30. It is in no release. 3.1.0-rc.1 still has the branch commented out.
- Tried `main` at 164c64b in a throwaway worktree. Needed: Git LFS archives materialized by hand in cargo's checkout (`git lfs smudge`), and `SessionInfo::cycles()` no longer exists. It built, guest included, with no Metal kernel errors. One proof of `usecase-document_cloud`, 7.9 million cycles, did not finish in 300 seconds. A second, sampled run was stopped by the human, whose GPU monitor showed only brief small spikes.
- Conclusion: no usable GPU proving for this backend on an Apple laptop today. Decision 0017 withdrawn, its vendored code removed. Server-side numbers need a CUDA machine.

Things worth reporting upstream: the `stacker` build failure on the guest
target (to Cedar or `stacker`), and the three ambiguous assignments in
`poseidon2.metal` under the macOS 27 Metal compiler (to RISC Zero).

### The comparison so far, with its limits

For one TinyTodo decision on this laptop:

- Circuit backend: proved in 2.2 seconds.
- zkVM backend: 23.3 million cycles, or 11.7 million without the schema. One CPU proof was stopped unfinished at 28 minutes.

This is not yet a fair comparison. The circuit opens two entities from a
Merkle root. The zkVM guest parses the whole store. E3's mode 1 would give the
guest the same two records, and then the two backends do the same work.

## Late night: E4, the compiler

Hours, assistant: circuit backend about 3 more, 5 in total. zkVM backend 4.5.
The human asked for the compiler, then browser and phone proving.

- The Term IR is reachable, by a side door. Decision 0019, point 1.
- The surprise of the day: the symbolic compiler's output for TinyTodo is, term for term, the circuit written by hand in E2. The front end does the typing, the error semantics and the per-action specialization. The back end is a few hundred lines.
- Surveying the corpus's terms before writing the translator changed the design. Real policies read set-valued attributes and nested records far more than TinyTodo suggests, so the record commitment was extended before any translation code was written.
- The translator ran correctly the first time on all 36 requests of six sets. That is either luck or evidence that a direct translation of a verified IR is hard to get wrong. More requests per set will say which.
- H4 is refuted at this version: 3 of 7 upstream policy sets. By term it is 95.5 percent, and one feature, the request context, is most of the gap.
